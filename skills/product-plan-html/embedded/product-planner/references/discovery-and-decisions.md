# Discovery and Decisions

## Establish the baseline

Read the nearest project instructions and existing product/design records. Inspect only connected behavior needed for the requested decision. Record sources and relevant observation dates or revisions. Use the session's browser routing and ownership rules for screen inspection.

Separate documented policy, observed code/runtime behavior, and inferred intent. A conflict is a finding, not permission to silently choose an authority or rewrite policy. Missing access is not evidence of absence. Continue independent work and identify the specific unavailable evidence.

## Decision loop

1. State the problem or unresolved decision in user language.
2. Reuse established answers and constraints.
3. If the outcome materially changes, compare plausible options using the same facts and constraints.
4. Give a recommendation with assumptions and tradeoffs, without fabricated scores.
5. Obtain the user's direction; update the affected specification or screen before the next major branch.

Ask one major decision at a time where practical. Do not wait for approval on every reversible detail or repeat an accepted choice. A user may combine alternatives or delegate the decision. A new assumption is not an accepted policy.

Record current decisions once in the authoritative document. When replacing a decision, update the current summary and retain a concise superseded note with the replacement reference. Avoid saving every conversational exchange.

## Research

Use available project evidence first. For requested external research or claims needing current verification, inspect appropriate sources through the permitted environment. Capture URL/source, date, observed fact, inference, applicability, and limitations. Never present a competitor feature as evidence that it will work for this product. Do not perform live user outreach or external writes without the required authorization.
