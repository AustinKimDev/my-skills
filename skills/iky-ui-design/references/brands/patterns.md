# Reference synthesis: patterns to adapt, not templates to obey

Read only the service evidence relevant to the current decision. These are design inferences from the linked observations, not measured outcomes or a closed set of styles. Compose across them or depart from all of them when the problem calls for it. The independent service files retain exact source URLs, dates, captures, and evidence limits.

## From evidence to an original direction

Describe the specific tension, identify what the reference resolves, then transform that mechanism for the task’s content, platform, and brand. A mixed workspace may borrow Toss’s label/action separation, Naver’s local controls, and Kakao’s continuity without resembling any of their whole screens. Consider a new spatial, temporal, or conversational structure when conventional page arrangements impose extra work. State why it may help and what observation would disprove the hypothesis.

For every recommendation identify what remains constant, what changes, what not to borrow, and what to verify. Ask a concrete question about the resulting tradeoff. Compare distinct hypotheses with the user’s established five-direction default; do not reuse the rows above as five mandatory themes.

## Evidence boundaries

The desktop captures do not validate mobile behavior; product illustrations do not validate native interactions. Historical material supplies rationale, not current UI specifications. Use the saved capture and observation for claims about the recorded screen. An unrecorded state transition or exact token remains unverified; research it only if the current task needs that evidence. Verify the implementation being delivered in its target environment. Do not treat a service’s logo color as its entire design language. Research capture assets are evidence, not automatically licensed product artwork.
