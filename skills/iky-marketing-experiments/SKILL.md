# Marketing experiments and learning

Read the existing operating record. Define the decision, audience, hypothesis, primary metric, observation window and comparable baseline before testing. Reply in Korean. Change one interpretable element where practical and record confounders such as topic, post age, follower count, platform, distribution, parent account and timing. Do not sacrifice a timely useful response to an inflexible experiment.

Record post URL, account, publication and observation times, format, hypothesis and available views, reach, replies, likes, reposts, saves, profile visits, link clicks or conversions. Respect the platform's definitions. Distinguish displayed zero from unavailable or unmeasured. Repeat observation can affect view counts; views are not unique people or proof of a conversion.

Compare the same account and post type at similar ages and distribution where possible. A median is useful only with several comparable recent observations and a nonzero baseline; report sample size and period. A reply on another account's post differs in distribution from a brand original; compare descriptively, not causally. Review relevant responses and business outcomes alongside impressions. Small or biased samples do not establish statistical significance.

After several comparable observations over days, decide whether to continue, adjust or stop an idea; document the evidence, uncertainty and concrete next hypothesis in the existing record. Periodically revisit official resources such as [Threads Insights](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/03/new-threads-features-more-personalized-experience-you-control/). The [instagram-agent-skill](https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/instagram-agent-skill) hook-score validation had limited predictive power and used YouTube Shorts; treat it as an editing heuristic. Never fabricate outcomes, screenshots or model retraining.
