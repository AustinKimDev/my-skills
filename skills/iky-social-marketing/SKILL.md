# Organic social marketing and learning

Grow the right audience through useful content and real conversations, not a posting quota. Use the user's account scope, brand voice, permissions, and existing operating record as the authority. Read [research notes](references/research.md) when setting a strategy, judging a hook or reviewing evidence. Reply to the user in Korean.

## Operate

1. Read the existing record, current permissions, recent published posts and scheduled content before deciding what to do. Use a designated agent-owned browser workspace and confirm the active account immediately before any authorized action. Respect a user's handoff and do not bypass a pending decision. Follow the actual platform and account constraints.
2. Discover actual questions and interests in mentions, following feeds, search and relevant public communities. Record source links and context. Community research does not authorize posting there. Contribute a small number of relevant, specific public replies when appropriate; do not manufacture personal experience or turn a reply into a pitch. Follow the user's posting and messaging limits.
3. Prepare distinct, audience-specific original content with a clear opening and concrete value: for example, a useful checklist, a grounded example, or one easy-to-answer question. Test media only when the user permits it and truthful assets are available; credit sources and do not present synthetic media as real customer evidence. Choose one primary action and avoid repeated generic promotion. Verify any product capability or launch claim.
4. If authorized and feasible, publish or schedule after checking conflicts and the user's cross-platform frequency limit. Otherwise record a usable draft and the actual blocker. Never call a draft or schedule a publication. The user and project determine approval requirements, not a source repository's generic workflow.
5. Record the actual action, source/post/reply links, precise time and available measurements, plus screenshots when feasible, in the existing authoritative log. Distinguish observed, unavailable and zero values. Keep private messages and personal data out of evidence. Report only meaningful changes or actions unless the user requests a status.

## Learn and decide

- Define a specific audience problem, content format, prediction, primary measure and observation window before each experiment. Track a useful secondary action such as a relevant reply, save, profile visit, link click or conversion only where the platform actually exposes it. Include reach/views and age at measurement, and record platform/account context.
- Compare like with like where possible: same account, post type, similar age, and comparable distribution. A reply to another account can have a very different audience from a brand's own post. Views can include repeat checks; do not treat them as unique people or proof of downstream conversion. Unavailable analytics are not zero.
- Use an account's own recent median only when there are enough comparable observations and the baseline is nonzero; never present this ratio as causal. Small or uneven samples support a tentative direction, not a winner. Assess several comparable posts over days, stop or revise a repeatedly weak idea, and record the next experiment and why. Do not claim significance or a fixed growth rate without supporting data.
- Revisit official platform guidance and relevant audience discussions periodically rather than rechecking the same numbers hourly. Distinguish platform claims, outside heuristics, and observed account results. Translate lessons into the next concrete post or reply; revise this skill only for a recurring, evidenced workflow improvement, and preserve the existing authorization boundaries.

Platform writing skills may support the copy when available. Follow the user's current channel-specific restrictions; this skill grants no new permission to post, send messages, spend money, change accounts, or install services.
